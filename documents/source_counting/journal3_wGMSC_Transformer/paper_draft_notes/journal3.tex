\begin{abstract}
  Accurately estimating the number of active sound sources in real-time is a fundamental prerequisite for multi-microphone acoustic signal processing tasks, such as source separation, localization, and assistive listening. Existing deep neural network (DNN)-based source counting methods often rely on long observation windows that introduce unacceptable latency for online applications, or they are strictly constrained to fixed microphone array geometries. In this paper, we propose a strictly STFT-frame causal, online acoustic sound source counting system that handles a variable number of input channels and dynamically adapts to arbitrary array geometries. Our approach leverages a causal Transformer-based architecture combined with robust spatial features, specifically the Whitened Generalized Magnitude Squared Coherence (GMSC). By employing time-reversed whitening, the network tracks both source activations and deactivations by measuring spatial coherence. Unlike traditional overlapped speech detection systems that rely on linguistic alignment, our model is trained on a purely acoustic power-based ground truth, allowing it to generalize to diverse, non-speech acoustic sources. To achieve array geometry agnosticism, the system utilizes an early attention-pooling mechanism to unify variable microphone counts, alongside a novel late-fusion strategy that injects physical microphone coordinates directly into the network via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) layers. We comprehensively evaluate the proposed geometry-conditioned system through zero-shot testing on both simulated environments and real-world datasets, including BRUDEX, CHiME-6, and AMI. Our results demonstrate the effectiveness of combining strictly causal Transformers with physical coordinate conditioning to achieve robust, real-time sound source counting across mismatched and dynamic array configurations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Signal Model and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:signal_model}

In this section, we formulate the acoustic signal model in the time and \gls{STFT} domains, describe the underlying second-order spatial statistics with \gls{ATF} vectors, and formally define the strictly causal frame-level sound source counting problem.

\subsection{Acoustic Signal Model in the Time and STFT Domains}
\label{sec:signal_model_stft}
We consider an acoustic scenario with $K_{\mathrm{max}}$ sound sources captured by an arbitrary array of $M$ microphones. Let $y_{m,l}$ denote the discrete-time signal recorded at the $m$-th microphone ($m \in \{1, \ldots, M\}$), sampled at a sampling frequency $f_s$, where $l \in \{1, \ldots, L\}$ denotes the discrete-time sample index and $L$ is the total number of samples in the considered signal segment.

By applying the \gls{STFT} with an analysis window of length $L_{\mathrm{win}}$ and a frame shift of $L_{\mathrm{hop}}$ samples, the microphone signals are transformed into the time-frequency domain. The total number of \gls{STFT} frames $T$ obtained from a segment of $L$ samples is given by
\begin{align}
  T = \left\lfloor \frac{L - L_{\mathrm{win}}}{L_{\mathrm{hop}}} \right\rfloor + 1.
  \label{eq:num_frames_def}
\end{align}
Discarding the non-informative DC ($f=0$) and Nyquist ($f=L_{\mathrm{win}}/2$) components yields $F = L_{\mathrm{win}}/2 - 1$ frequency bins. Stacking the \gls{STFT} coefficients of all $M$ microphones yields the multi-channel observation vector:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{y}_{t,f} = \begin{bmatrix} y_{1,t,f} & y_{2,t,f} & \cdots & y_{M,t,f} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times 1},
  \label{eq:sig_vec_def}
\end{align}
where $t \in \{1, \ldots, T\}$ and $f \in \{1, \ldots, F\}$ denote the time frame index and frequency bin index, respectively, and $\{\cdot\}^{\mathrm{T}}$ denotes the transpose operator. For notational conciseness, the frequency bin index $f$ is omitted in the remainder of this section (i.e., $\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{y}_{t,f}$) whenever narrowband operations are implied.

At each time frame $t$, the multi-microphone signal $\mathbf{y}_t$ is composed of the superposition of the acoustic contributions from all currently active sound sources and an additive ambient noise component $\mathbf{n}_t \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}$, i.e.,
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{y}_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K_t} \mathbf{x}_{k,t} + \mathbf{n}_t,
  \label{eq:acoustic_mixture_model}
\end{align}
where $K_t \in \{0, 1, \ldots, K_{\mathrm{max}}\}$ denotes the instantaneous number of active sound sources at time frame $t$. Importantly, $K_t$ is a broadband quantity (i.e., source activity is assumed to be frequency-independent across all frequency bins), and $\mathbf{x}_{k,t} \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}$ denotes the spatial component of the $k$-th sound source.

Under the \gls{MTF} approximation~\cite{avargel_multiplicative_2007}, the spatial source component vector $\mathbf{x}_{k,t}$ can be expressed as the product of the emitted source signal $s_{k,t} \in \mathbb{C}$ and the (time-invariant) \gls{ATF} vector $\mathbf{a}_k \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times 1}$, i.e.,
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{x}_{k,t} = \mathbf{a}_k s_{k,t} \quad \forall k \in \{1, \ldots, K_t\},
  \label{eq:atf_source_component}
\end{align}
with the \gls{ATF} vector of the $k$-th sound source defined as
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{a}_k = \begin{bmatrix} a_{1,k} & a_{2,k} & \cdots & a_{M,k} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times 1},
\end{align}
where $a_{m,k}$ represents the acoustic transfer function relating source $k$ to the $m$-th microphone. We assume that the \gls{ATF} vectors of all sources are linearly independent.
By collecting the \gls{ATF} vectors of the $K_t$ active sources into the mixing matrix
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{A}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{a}_1 & \mathbf{a}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{a}_{K_t} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times K_t},
\end{align}
and defining the source signal vector $\mathbf{s}_t = \begin{bmatrix} s_{1,t} & \cdots & s_{K_t,t} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{C}^{K_t \times 1}$, the multi-microphone observation vector in~\Cref{eq:acoustic_mixture_model} can be compactly written as:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{A}_t \mathbf{s}_t + \mathbf{n}_t.
  \label{eq:matrix_mixture_model}
\end{align}

\subsection{Second-Order Spatial Statistics}
\label{sec:second_order_statistics}
Assuming that the individual sound sources are mutually uncorrelated and uncorrelated with the additive ambient noise, the spatial covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}_{y,t} \in \mathbb{C}^{M\times M}$ of the noisy microphone signals at time frame $t$ is given by
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{R}_{y,t} = \mathbb{E}\left\{ \mathbf{y}_t \mathbf{y}_t^{\mathrm{H}} \right\} = \mathbf{R}_{x,t} + \mathbf{R}_{n,t},
  \label{eq:cov_model}
\end{align}
where $\mathbb{E}\{\cdot\}$ denotes the expectation operator, $\{\cdot\}^{\mathrm{H}}$ denotes the conjugate transpose operator, $\mathbf{R}_{n,t} = \mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{n}_t \mathbf{n}_t^{\mathrm{H}}\}$ denotes the noise covariance matrix, and $\mathbf{R}_{x,t} = \mathbb{E}\{\mathbf{x}_t \mathbf{x}_t^{\mathrm{H}}\}$ denotes the spatial covariance matrix of the sound sources, with $\mathbf{x}_t = \sum_{k=1}^{K_t}\mathbf{x}_{k,t}$. Substituting~\Cref{eq:matrix_mixture_model} into~\Cref{eq:cov_model} yields:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{R}_{x,t} = \mathbf{A}_t \bm{\Phi}_{s,t} \mathbf{A}_t^{\mathrm{H}} = \sum_{k=1}^{K_t} \phi_{s,k,t} \, \mathbf{a}_k \mathbf{a}_k^{\mathrm{H}},
  \label{eq:clean_cov_model}
\end{align}
where $\bm{\Phi}_{s,t} = \mathrm{diag}\left\{ \begin{bmatrix} \phi_{s,1,t} & \cdots & \phi_{s,K_t,t} \end{bmatrix} \right\} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{K_t \times K_t}$ is a diagonal matrix containing the time-varying power spectral densities $\phi_{s,k,t} = \mathbb{E}\{|s_{k,t}|^2\} > 0$ of the actively contributing sound sources.

Because the \gls{ATF} vectors $\mathbf{a}_k$ are assumed to be linearly independent and the active source powers are strictly non-zero, the rank of the clean spatial covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}_{x,t}$ in~\Cref{eq:clean_cov_model} is directly determined by the number of active sound sources, i.e.,
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{rank}\{\mathbf{R}_{x,t}\} = \min(M, K_t),
  \label{eq:rank_relation}
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{rank}\{\cdot\}$ denotes the matrix rank operator.
This fundamental algebraic relationship between source multiplicity and spatial covariance matrix rank provides the theoretical foundation for multi-channel spatial analysis and spatial coherence metrics.

\subsection{Online Sound Source Counting Framework}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}
The core objective of this work is to estimate the instantaneous number of active sound sources $K_t$ at each time frame $t$ from the multi-channel acoustic observations. Formally, we define the online sound source counting estimator $\mathcal{C}$ as a mapping from the multi-channel time-frequency observations to the estimated instantaneous source count $\widehat{K}_t$:
\begin{align}
  \widehat{K}_t = \mathcal{C}\left( \left\{ \mathbf{y}_{\tau, f} \right\}_{\tau=1, f=1}^{t, F} \right) \quad \forall t \in \{1, \ldots, T\}.
  \label{eq:strict_causality_definition}
\end{align}
To enable low-latency operation in real-time acoustic applications, we impose a strict \gls{STFT} frame-level causality constraint: the estimation at time frame $t$ is conditioned exclusively on current and past observations up to frame $t$ ($\tau \leq t$), with zero look-ahead into future frames ($\tau > t$). All neural network architectures evaluated in this work are designed to strictly comply with~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition}.

We formulate source counting as an integer classification problem with $(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1)$ mutually exclusive classes, where $K_t = 0$ corresponds to source absence (ambient noise only). For a given audio segment comprising $T$ time frames, the ground-truth source count sequence is represented as a $T$-dimensional target vector:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{k} = \begin{bmatrix} K_1 & K_2 & \cdots & K_T \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \{0, 1, \ldots, K_{\mathrm{max}}\}^{T}.
  \label{eq:target_vector}
\end{align}

It is essential to distinguish the acoustic ground truth used in this paper from conventional text-driven linguistic alignments commonly found in the speech diarization literature. Traditional diarization and voice activity detection benchmarks often define ground-truth activity using linguistic forced-alignment tools (e.g., the Montreal Forced Aligner~\cite{mcauliffe2017montreal}), which label speech activity strictly based on phonetically transcribed text. Consequently, non-linguistic vocalizations (such as coughs, throat clearings, laughter, and heavy breathing) as well as non-speech acoustic sound events are labeled as inactive, even though substantial directional acoustic power impinges upon the microphone array. In contrast, in this work we define $K_t$ based on \emph{acoustic sound presence}: any sound source producing energy above an acoustic presence threshold relative to the background noise is classified as active, ensuring the model reflects the physical reality of the sound field.

\Cref{fig:system_overview} illustrates the general processing pipeline of the online sound source counting estimators investigated in this work. \section{Feature Extraction}
\label{sec:feature_extraction}

In accordance with~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition}, all feature extractors evaluated in this work are strictly causal. \begin{align}
  C \times D \times T,

\end{align}

\subsection{Spectral Features}
\label{sec:spectral_features}
Spectral features characterize the frequency-domain energy distribution and harmonic structure of the acoustic scene, extracted either from a single designated reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}} \in \{1, \ldots, M\}$ or across all $M$ microphone channels. In the following, we describe the \gls{STFT} representation and Log-Mel filterbank energies in both their single-channel and multi-channel forms.

\subsubsection{STFT Representation}
\label{sec:feat_stft}
Stacking the multi-channel observation vectors $\mathbf{y}_{t,f} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times 1}$ from~\Cref{eq:sig_vec_def} along the frequency and time dimensions yields the complex time-frequency tensor $\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times F \times T}$, with scalar coefficients $y_{m,t,f} \in \mathbb{C}$. To represent these complex coefficients in real-valued neural networks while avoiding wrap-around discontinuities of the phase angle around $\pm\pi$, each complex coefficient is represented in polar form by decomposing it into its magnitude $|y_{m,t,f}| \in [0, \infty)$ and phase angle $\varphi_{m,t,f} = \arg\{y_{m,t,f}\} \in (-\pi, \pi]$, and continuously mapping the phase angle onto the unit circle via cosine and sine functions:
\begin{align}
  y_{m,t,f} = |y_{m,t,f}| \, e^{\mathrm{i} \varphi_{m,t,f}},
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{i} = \sqrt{-1}$ is the imaginary unit. Concatenating the magnitude, cosine phase, and sine phase across all $F$ frequency bins yields the polar feature vector
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{m,t}^{(\mathrm{STFT})} = \begin{bmatrix} |\mathbf{y}_{m,t}| \\ \cos\{\bm{\varphi}_{m,t}\} \\ \sin\{\bm{\varphi}_{m,t}\} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{3F \times 1},
  \label{eq:feat_stft_vec}
\end{align}
where $|\mathbf{y}_{m,t}| = [|y_{m,t,1}|, \ldots, |y_{m,t,F}|]^{\mathrm{T}} \in [0, \infty)^{F \times 1}$ denotes the element-wise magnitude spectrum vector and $\bm{\varphi}_{m,t} = [\varphi_{m,t,1}, \ldots, \varphi_{m,t,F}]^{\mathrm{T}} \in (-\pi, \pi]^{F \times 1}$ denotes the phase angle vector, with $\cos\{\cdot\}$ and $\sin\{\cdot\}$ applied element-wise.
Extracting $\mathbf{z}_{m_{\mathrm{ref}},t}^{(\mathrm{STFT})}$ exclusively from the reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ across all $T$ time frames yields the single-channel \gls{STFT} feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}}^{(\mathrm{ref})} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 3F \times T},
  \label{eq:feat_stft_ref_tensor}
\end{align}
whereas stacking $\mathbf{z}_{m,t}^{(\mathrm{STFT})}$ across all $M$ microphones yields the multi-channel \gls{STFT} feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 3F \times T}.
  \label{eq:feat_stft_tensor}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Log-Mel Filterbank Energies}
\label{sec:feat_logmel}
As an alternative to raw spectral coefficients, Log-Mel filterbank energies provide a lower-dimensional representation of spectral envelopes and harmonicity that models the frequency resolution of the human auditory system. This serves as the primary spectral feature representation in Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}.

For microphone $m$ at time frame $t$, the short-time power spectrum is defined as the squared magnitude vector $\mathbf{p}_{y,m,t} = [|y_{m,t,1}|^2, \ldots, |y_{m,t,F}|^2]^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{F \times 1}$. To compress the linear frequency axis onto the Mel scale, a filterbank weighting matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{mel}} \in \mathbb{R}_{+}^{D_{\mathrm{mel}} \times F}$ comprising $D_{\mathrm{mel}}$ overlapping triangular bandpass filters is applied. Subsequent logarithmic dynamic range compression yields the Log-Mel energy vector
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{m,t}^{(\mathrm{mel})} = \log\left\{ \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{mel}} \mathbf{p}_{y,m,t} + \epsilon \right\} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{mel}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:feat_logmel_vec}
\end{align}
where $\log\{\cdot\}$ is applied element-wise and $\epsilon > 0$ is a small constant ensuring numerical stability. Analogously to the \gls{STFT} representation, extracting $\mathbf{z}_{m_{\mathrm{ref}},t}^{(\mathrm{mel})}$ solely from the reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ across all $T$ time frames yields the single-channel Log-Mel feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}}^{(\mathrm{ref})} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D_{\mathrm{mel}} \times T},
  \label{eq:feat_logmel_ref_tensor}
\end{align}
whereas stacking $\mathbf{z}_{m,t}^{(\mathrm{mel})}$ across all $M$ microphones yields the multi-channel Log-Mel feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times D_{\mathrm{mel}} \times T}.
  \label{eq:feat_logmel_tensor}
\end{align}

\subsection{Spatial Features}
\label{sec:feat_spatial}
While spectral features characterize the time-frequency energy distribution and harmonic structure of individual channels, spatial features capture inter-channel phase and coherence relationships that encode source positions and spatial diversity across the microphone array.

\subsubsection{Cosine and Sine Inter-Channel Phase Differences (CSIPD)}
\label{sec:feat_csipd}
Inter-channel phase differences characterize the relative time delays of acoustic wavefronts arriving at pairs of microphones. Let $\mathcal{P} \subseteq \{(i, j) \mid 1 \le i < j \le M\}$ denote a set of $P = |\mathcal{P}| = (M(M-1))/2$ microphone pairs. For a given pair $p = (i, j) \in \mathcal{P}$ at time frame $t$ and frequency bin $f$, the \gls{IPD} is defined as the wrapped phase difference between the respective \gls{STFT} coefficients:
\begin{align}
  \theta_{p,t,f} = \arg\{y_{i,t,f} y_{j,t,f}^*\} \in (-\pi, \pi],
  \label{eq:feat_ipd_scalar}
\end{align}
where $\{\cdot\}^*$ denotes complex conjugation. Collecting the phase differences across all $F$ frequency bins yields the phase difference vector
\begin{align}
  \bm{\theta}_{p,t} = \begin{bmatrix} \theta_{p,t,1} & \cdots & \theta_{p,t,F} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in (-\pi, \pi]^{F \times 1}.
  \label{eq:feat_ipd_vec}
\end{align}
Because the cyclic nature of phase values introduces artificial discontinuities around $\pm\pi$, the \gls{CSIPD} representation projects $\bm{\theta}_{p,t}$ onto the unit circle via trigonometric expansion~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{p,t}^{(\mathrm{CSIPD})} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\{\bm{\theta}_{p,t}\} \\ \sin\{\bm{\theta}_{p,t}\} \end{bmatrix} \in [-1, 1]^{2F \times 1},
  \label{eq:feat_csipd_vec}
\end{align}
where $\cos\{\cdot\}$ and $\sin\{\cdot\}$ are applied element-wise. Beyond eliminating phase wrapping ambiguities, an essential property of \gls{CSIPD} features is that both the generalized cross-correlation with phase transform (GCC-PHAT) and the steered response power (SRP-PHAT) angular spectra can be represented as linear transformations of $\mathbf{z}_{p,t}^{(\mathrm{CSIPD})}$~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}. Consequently, downstream neural network layers can implicitly synthesize spatial steering responses without requiring explicit angular grid evaluations.
Stacking across all $P$ microphone pairs and $T$ time frames yields the spatial feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{CSIPD}} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times 2F \times T}.
  \label{eq:feat_csipd_tensor}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Spatial Coherence}
\label{sec:feat_coherence}
\label{sec:feat_gmsc}
Beyond inter-channel phase differences, the spatial coherence of the sound field provides crucial information about the acoustic scene that directly relates to the source count $K_t$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diffuse Noise ($K_t = 0$):} In a diffuse noise field, the acoustic signals at different microphones are largely uncorrelated, resulting in low spatial coherence.
  \item \textbf{Single Active Source ($K_t = 1$):} When a single localized source is active, its \gls{ATF} vector $\mathbf{a}_k$ induces a dominant rank-1 component in~\Cref{eq:clean_cov_model}, resulting in high spatial coherence.
  \item \textbf{Multiple Active Sources ($K_t \ge 2$):} When multiple concurrent localized sources are active, the superimposition of multiple linearly independent \gls{ATF} vectors destroys this directional structure, causing the sound field to appear spatially incoherent.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Pairwise Spatial Coherence (PSC)}
To evaluate spatial coherence across the microphone array, the short-time spatial covariance matrix of the microphone observation vector $\mathbf{y}_{t,f} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times 1}$ is recursively estimated at each time frame $t$ and frequency bin $f$ as
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f} = \alpha \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t-1,f} + (1-\alpha) \mathbf{y}_{t,f} \mathbf{y}_{t,f}^{\mathrm{H}},
  \label{eq:cov_est_rec}
\end{align}
where the forgetting factor $\alpha = e^{-\nicefrac{\tau_{\mathrm{fs}}}{\tau_{\alpha}}}$ is parameterized by the \gls{STFT} frame shift $\tau_{\mathrm{fs}} = \nicefrac{L_{\mathrm{hop}}}{f_s}$ and a smoothing time constant $\tau_{\alpha} > 0$, and $\{\cdot\}^{\mathrm{H}}$ denotes the conjugate transpose. Normalizing $\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}$ by its diagonal power entries yields the spatial coherence matrix:
\begin{align}
  \bm{\Gamma}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\} = (\mathrm{diag}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\})^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}} \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f} (\mathrm{diag}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\})^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}},
  \label{eq:coherence_operator}
\end{align}
where $\mathrm{diag}\{\cdot\}$ forms a diagonal matrix containing the diagonal entries of the argument. The off-diagonal entries of $\bm{\Gamma}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\}$ correspond to the pairwise complex spatial coherence values between individual microphone pairs. For each microphone pair $p = (i, j) \in \mathcal{P}$, the complex coherence coefficient is defined as
\begin{align}
  \Gamma_{p,t,f} = [\bm{\Gamma}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\}]_{i,j} = \frac{[\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}]_{i,j}}{\sqrt{[\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}]_{i,i} [\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}]_{j,j}}} \in \mathbb{C},
  \label{eq:feat_psc_scalar}
\end{align}
where $[\cdot]_{i,j}$ denotes the scalar entry in row $i$ and column $j$ of a matrix, satisfying $|\Gamma_{p,t,f}| \le 1$, with phase angle $\psi_{p,t,f} = \arg\{\Gamma_{p,t,f}\} \in (-\pi, \pi]$. We represent $\Gamma_{p,t,f}$ in polar form by decomposing it into its magnitude $|\Gamma_{p,t,f}| \in [0, 1]$ and phase angle $\psi_{p,t,f}$, continuously mapping the phase angle onto the unit circle via cosine and sine functions to eliminate wrap-around discontinuities at $\pm\pi$. Concatenating magnitude, cosine phase, and sine phase across all $F$ frequency bins yields the polar feature vector
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{p,t}^{(\mathrm{PSC})} = \begin{bmatrix} |\bm{\gamma}_{p,t}| \\ \cos\{\bm{\psi}_{p,t}\} \\ \sin\{\bm{\psi}_{p,t}\} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{3F \times 1},
  \label{eq:feat_psc_vec}
\end{align}
where $|\bm{\gamma}_{p,t}| = [|\Gamma_{p,t,1}|, \dots, |\Gamma_{p,t,F}|]^{\mathrm{T}} \in [0, 1]^{F \times 1}$ and $\bm{\psi}_{p,t} = [\psi_{p,t,1}, \dots, \psi_{p,t,F}]^{\mathrm{T}} \in (-\pi, \pi]^{F \times 1}$, with trigonometric functions applied element-wise.
Stacking across all $P$ microphone pairs and $T$ time frames yields the spatial feature tensor
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{PSC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times 3F \times T}.
  \label{eq:feat_psc_tensor}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Mathematical Definition of GMSC}
While \gls{PSC} evaluates coherence across all $P$ microphone pairs, yielding a pair-dependent representation that scales quadratically with the microphone count $M$, the \gls{GMSC}~\cite{gmsc_ramirez_2008,gode2026dnnbased} generalizes pairwise coherence by condensing the entire multi-channel spatial coherence matrix $\bm{\Gamma}\{\mathbf{R}\}$ into a single scalar coherence metric per frequency bin independent of $M$, i.e., $C=1$. Specifically, for any positive semi-definite spatial covariance matrix $\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{C}^{M \times M}$ and its associated coherence matrix $\bm{\Gamma}\{\mathbf{R}\}$ from~\Cref{eq:coherence_operator}, the \gls{GMSC} operator is defined via principal eigenvalue extraction:
\begin{align}
  \operatorname{gmsc}\{\mathbf{R}\} &= \frac{\lambda_{\max}\{\bm{\Gamma}\{\mathbf{R}\}\} - 1}{M - 1} \in [0, 1], \label{eq:gmsc_operator}
\end{align}
where $\lambda_{\max}\{\cdot\}$ denotes the principal eigenvalue, with $0$ indicating low spatial coherence and $1$ indicating high spatial coherence.

\paragraph{Standard GMSC}
Evaluating~\Cref{eq:gmsc_operator} directly on the short-time spatial covariance matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}$ from~\Cref{eq:cov_est_rec} yields the \gls{GMSC} coefficient $\gamma_{y,t,f} = \operatorname{gmsc}\{\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}\}$. Collecting values across all $F$ frequency bins yields the \gls{GMSC} feature vector:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{t}^{(\mathrm{GMSC})} = \begin{bmatrix} \gamma_{y,t,1} & \cdots & \gamma_{y,t,F} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in [0, 1]^{F \times 1}.
  \label{eq:feat_gmsc_vec}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Whitened GMSC (wGMSC)}
Because mixtures of multiple concurrent sources exhibit low coherence similar to diffuse noise, standard \gls{GMSC} cannot detect source transitions in ongoing multi-source scenarios. To resolve this ambiguity, the \gls{wGMSC}~\cite{gode2026dnnbased} whitens the sound field with a reference covariance matrix containing the already active sources. This effectively converts all previously active sources into spatially white noise, such that any newly activating source acts as a single localized source against a white background, generating a prominent coherence peak:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Source Activation Feature}: To detect newly activating sources, $\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}$ is whitened against a reference covariance matrix $\mathbf{R}_{v,t,f} = \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t-T_v,f}$ taken from a causal lookback window of $T_v$ frames:
  \begin{align}
    \mathbf{R}_{w,t,f}^{(\mathrm{a})} = \mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}^{-\nicefrac{\mathrm{H}}{2}} \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f} \mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}},
    \label{eq:whitening_act}
  \end{align}
  where the matrix square-root factor $\mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}^{\nicefrac{1}{2}}$ is defined such that $\mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}^{\nicefrac{\mathrm{H}}{2}}\mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}^{\nicefrac{1}{2}} = \mathbf{R}_{v,t,f}$ (e.g., Cholesky decomposition). Evaluating~\Cref{eq:gmsc_operator} yields the activation \gls{GMSC} $\gamma_{t,f}^{(\mathrm{a})} = \operatorname{gmsc}\{\mathbf{R}_{w,t,f}^{(\mathrm{a})}\} \in [0, 1]$. Stacking across frequency yields $\bm{\gamma}_{t}^{(\mathrm{a})} = [\gamma_{t,1}^{(\mathrm{a})}, \dots, \gamma_{t,F}^{(\mathrm{a})}]^{\mathrm{T}} \in [0, 1]^{F \times 1}$.
  
  \item \textbf{Source Deactivation Feature}: To track sources ceasing activity while strictly maintaining causality, source deactivations are modeled as time-reversed activations~\cite{gode2026dnnbased}. In this formulation, the past covariance matrix contains the deactivated source, while the current covariance matrix does not. To avoid recursive tail leakage from the deactivated source into the current estimate, covariance matrices are estimated via a rectangular moving-average window of length $T_{\mathrm{ma}} \le T_v$:
  \begin{align}
    \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}^{(\mathrm{ma})} = \frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{ma}}} \sum_{\tau=0}^{T_{\mathrm{ma}}-1} \mathbf{y}_{t-\tau,f} \mathbf{y}_{t-\tau,f}^{\mathrm{H}}.
    \label{eq:cov_est_ma}
  \end{align}
  Whitening the past covariance matrix with the current covariance matrix yields:
  \begin{align}
    \mathbf{R}_{w,t,f}^{(\mathrm{d})} = \left(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}^{(\mathrm{ma})}\right)^{-\nicefrac{\mathrm{H}}{2}} \widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t-T_v,f}^{(\mathrm{ma})} \left(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{y,t,f}^{(\mathrm{ma})}\right)^{-\nicefrac{1}{2}}.
    \label{eq:whitening_deact}
  \end{align}
  The deactivation \gls{GMSC} is evaluated as $\gamma_{t,f}^{(\mathrm{d})} = \operatorname{gmsc}\{\mathbf{R}_{w,t,f}^{(\mathrm{d})}\} \in [0, 1]$, yielding the deactivation vector $\bm{\gamma}_{t}^{(\mathrm{d})} = [\gamma_{t,1}^{(\mathrm{d})}, \dots, \gamma_{t,F}^{(\mathrm{d})}]^{\mathrm{T}} \in [0, 1]^{F \times 1}$. Because this deactivation feature relies strictly on observations $\tau \le t$, causality is preserved, with an inherent processing delay of $T_v$ frames after a deactivation occurs.
\end{enumerate}
Concatenating the activation and deactivation feature vectors yields the bidirectional \gls{wGMSC} feature vector:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_{t}^{(\mathrm{wGMSC})} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\gamma}_{t}^{(\mathrm{a})} \\ \bm{\gamma}_{t}^{(\mathrm{d})} \end{bmatrix} \in [0, 1]^{2F \times 1}.
  \label{eq:feat_wgmsc_vec}
\end{align}

Stacking across all $T$ time frames yields the real-valued spatial feature tensors:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{GMSC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times F \times T}, \quad \mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{wGMSC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times 2F \times T},
  \label{eq:feat_gmsc_tensors}
\end{align}
respectively.

Our neural network framework builds directly upon the Transformer architecture introduced by Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}. We first formulate the exact baseline architecture of~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} for a single-stream input representation ($C = 1$ in~\Cref{eq:tensor_shape_convention}), which incorporates local and segment-level future context, and subsequently detail the modifications required to obtain a strictly causal online estimator in compliance with~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition} (\Cref{sec:strictly_causal_adaptation}). Here, a single-stream representation corresponds either to single-channel spectral features extracted from a reference microphone (e.g., $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$ or $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$), to pairwise spatial features flattened across microphone pairs, or to an array-condensed spatial coherence representation ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{GMSC}}$ or $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{wGMSC}}$). Let
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Z} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_1 & \mathbf{z}_2 & \cdots & \mathbf{z}_T \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}
  \label{eq:single_stream_input}
\end{align}
denote the input feature matrix over $T$ \gls{STFT} time frames, where $\mathbf{z}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times 1}$ is the feature vector at time frame $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$. As illustrated in~\Cref{fig:transformer_architecture}, the single-stream Transformer architecture processes $\mathbf{Z}$ through four sequential stages: context concatenation and temporal subsampling (cat-pool), bottleneck projection with sinusoidal \gls{PE}, a stack of $R$ Pre-\gls{LN} \gls{TE} blocks, and a classification output stage with temporal sequence upsampling.

\subsubsection{Context Concatenation and Temporal Subsampling}
\label{sec:causal_cat_pool}
Because the computational complexity and memory footprint of self-attention scale quadratically as $\mathcal{O}(T^2)$ with the sequence length $T$, Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} introduced a concatenate-subsample (cat-pool) front-end that transfers local temporal context into the feature dimension prior to downsampling the frame axis.

In the baseline formulation of~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}, at each time frame $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$, the current feature vector $\mathbf{z}_t$ is stacked with a symmetric bidirectional context window comprising $T_{\mathrm{c}} \in \mathbb{N}_0$ past frames and $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ future frames (denoted as $C$ in~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}):
\begin{align}
  \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_t = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_{t - T_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\mathrm{T}} & \cdots & \mathbf{z}_{t}^{\mathrm{T}} & \cdots & \mathbf{z}_{t + T_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\mathrm{T}} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:causal_cat}
\end{align}
where $D_{\mathrm{cat}} = N_{\mathrm{ctx}} D$ with $N_{\mathrm{ctx}} = 2T_{\mathrm{c}} + 1$ denotes the concatenated feature dimension, and boundary frames outside the segment ($\tau \le 0$ or $\tau > T$) are zero-padded as $\mathbf{z}_{\tau} = \mathbf{0}_{D \times 1}$. The stacked sequence is then subsampled along the temporal axis by a temporal subsampling factor $T_{\mathrm{sub}} \in \mathbb{N}_+$ (denoted as $S$ in~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}), yielding $T' = \lfloor (T - 1) / T_{\mathrm{sub}} \rfloor + 1$ subsampled frames:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{u}_{t'} = \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_{(t'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat}} \times 1}, \quad \forall t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\},
  \label{eq:causal_pool}
\end{align}
where $t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}$ denotes the subsampled time frame index, forming $\mathbf{U} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{u}_1 & \cdots & \mathbf{u}_{T'} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat}} \times T'}$. In~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}, the default configuration uses $T_{\mathrm{c}} = 7$ (stacking $N_{\mathrm{ctx}} = 15$ contiguous frames) and $T_{\mathrm{sub}} = 10$, which reduces self-attention complexity by a factor of $T_{\mathrm{sub}}^2 = 100$ while retaining full coverage of intermediate frames because $2T_{\mathrm{c}} + 1 \ge T_{\mathrm{sub}}$. Setting $T_{\mathrm{sub}} = 1$ disables temporal subsampling ($T' = T$ and $\mathbf{u}_t = \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_t$), whereas setting $T_{\mathrm{c}} = 0$ disables context stacking ($D_{\mathrm{cat}} = D$).

\subsubsection{Input Normalization, Bottleneck Projection, and Positional Encoding}
\label{sec:bottleneck_pe}
Following the cat-pool stage, each vector $\mathbf{u}_{t'}$ is normalized across its feature dimension via \gls{LN}~\cite{ba2016layer}. For an arbitrary vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathbf{v}} \times 1}$ with $d$-th scalar entry $[\mathbf{v}]_d$, the \gls{LN} operator is defined as
\begin{align}
  \mathrm{LN}\{\mathbf{v}\} = \bm{\gamma}_{\mathrm{LN}} \odot \frac{\mathbf{v} - \mu_{\mathbf{v}} \mathbf{1}_{D_{\mathbf{v}}}}{\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathbf{v}}^2 + \epsilon_{\mathrm{LN}}}} + \bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{LN}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathbf{v}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:layernorm_def}
\end{align}
where $\mu_{\mathbf{v}} = \frac{1}{D_{\mathbf{v}}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{\mathbf{v}}} [\mathbf{v}]_d$ and $\sigma_{\mathbf{v}}^2 = \frac{1}{D_{\mathbf{v}}}\sum_{d=1}^{D_{\mathbf{v}}} ([\mathbf{v}]_d - \mu_{\mathbf{v}})^2$ denote the sample mean and variance across the $D_{\mathbf{v}}$ feature entries, $\mathbf{1}_{D_{\mathbf{v}}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathbf{v}} \times 1}$ is the all-ones vector, $\bm{\gamma}_{\mathrm{LN}}, \bm{\beta}_{\mathrm{LN}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathbf{v}} \times 1}$ are learnable affine gain and bias parameters, $\epsilon_{\mathrm{LN}} > 0$ is a small constant for numerical stability, and $\odot$ denotes the element-wise Hadamard product.

Subsequently, a bottleneck point-wise ($1 \times 1$) convolution maps the normalized $D_{\mathrm{cat}}$-dimensional vector at each subsampled frame $t'$ to a compact model embedding dimension $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0,\mathrm{in})} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}} \, \mathrm{LN}\{\mathbf{u}_{t'}\} + \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:bottleneck_conv}
\end{align}
with learnable weight matrix $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times D_{\mathrm{cat}}}$ and bias vector $\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$. Because self-attention does not inherently encode sequential order, deterministic sinusoidal \gls{PE} vectors $\mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$~\cite{vaswani2017attention} are added to the bottleneck embeddings:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0)} = \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0,\mathrm{in})} + \mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}, \quad \forall t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\},
  \label{eq:pos_enc_add}
\end{align}
where the entries of $\mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})}$ for dimension index $d \in \{0, 1, \dots, D_{\mathrm{emb}}/2 - 1\}$ are given by
\begin{align}
  \left[\mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})}\right]_{2d+1} &= \sin\!\left\{ \frac{t' - 1}{10000^{\nicefrac{2d}{D_{\mathrm{emb}}}}} \right\}, \label{eq:pe_sin} \\
  \left[\mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})}\right]_{2d+2} &= \cos\!\left\{ \frac{t' - 1}{10000^{\nicefrac{2d}{D_{\mathrm{emb}}}}} \right\}. \label{eq:pe_cos}
\end{align}
Collecting the vectors $\mathbf{p}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{PE})}$ and $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0)}$ across all $T'$ subsampled frames yields the positional encoding matrix $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{PE}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{p}_1^{(\mathrm{PE})} & \cdots & \mathbf{p}_{T'}^{(\mathrm{PE})} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$ and the initial embedding matrix $\mathbf{H}^{(0)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{h}_1^{(0)} & \cdots & \mathbf{h}_{T'}^{(0)} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$.

\subsubsection{Pre-LN Transformer Encoder Blocks}
\label{sec:transformer_encoder_blocks}
The embedding matrix $\mathbf{H}^{(0)}$ is processed by a stack of $R$ identical \gls{TE} blocks. Following~\cite{cornell2022overlapped,nguyen2019transformers}, each block $r \in \{1, \dots, R\}$ adopts a Pre-\gls{LN} residual configuration, in which \gls{LN} is applied at the input of each residual branch rather than after the residual addition to improve gradient flow and training stability. As shown in the bottom panel of~\Cref{fig:transformer_architecture}, the $r$-th block maps $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{h}_1^{(r-1)} & \cdots & \mathbf{h}_{T'}^{(r-1)} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$ to $\mathbf{H}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$ via two sequential sub-blocks:

\paragraph{Multi-Head Self-Attention Sub-Block}
First, frame-wise \gls{LN} is applied to $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1)}$, yielding $\bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathrm{LN}\{\mathbf{h}_1^{(r-1)}\} & \cdots & \mathrm{LN}\{\mathbf{h}_{T'}^{(r-1)}\} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$. The \gls{MHA} layer partitions the $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$-dimensional embedding space into $N_{\mathrm{head}}$ parallel attention heads of dimension $D_{\mathrm{head}} = D_{\mathrm{emb}} / N_{\mathrm{head}}$. For each attention head $h \in \{1, \dots, N_{\mathrm{head}}\}$, the normalized sequence $\bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)}$ is linearly projected into query, key, and value matrices:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{Q}_h^{(r)} &= \mathbf{W}_{Q,h}^{(r)} \bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)} + \mathbf{b}_{Q,h}^{(r)} \mathbf{1}_{T'}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times T'}, \label{eq:mha_q} \\
  \mathbf{K}_h^{(r)} &= \mathbf{W}_{K,h}^{(r)} \bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)} + \mathbf{b}_{K,h}^{(r)} \mathbf{1}_{T'}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times T'}, \label{eq:mha_k} \\
  \mathbf{V}_h^{(r)} &= \mathbf{W}_{V,h}^{(r)} \bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)} + \mathbf{b}_{V,h}^{(r)} \mathbf{1}_{T'}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times T'}, \label{eq:mha_v}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_{Q,h}^{(r)}, \mathbf{W}_{K,h}^{(r)}, \mathbf{W}_{V,h}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times D_{\mathrm{emb}}}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{Q,h}^{(r)}, \mathbf{b}_{K,h}^{(r)}, \mathbf{b}_{V,h}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times 1}$ are learnable parameters.

In the baseline formulation of Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}, self-attention operates bidirectionally across the entire segment without causal masking, allowing the query at subsampled frame $t'$ to attend to key and value vectors from all past, current, and future subsampled frames $\tau' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}$. The unmasked scaled dot-product score matrix for head $h$ is given by
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{S}_h^{(r)} = \frac{\left(\mathbf{K}_h^{(r)}\right)^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{Q}_h^{(r)}}{\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{head}}}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T' \times T'}.
  \label{eq:attention_scores}
\end{align}
Applying the softmax function along each column $t'$ of $\mathbf{S}_h^{(r)}$ yields the bidirectional attention weight matrix $\mathbf{A}_h^{(r)} \in [0, 1]^{T' \times T'}$, whose $(\tau', t')$-th entry is
\begin{align}
  [\mathbf{A}_h^{(r)}]_{\tau', t'} = \frac{\exp\!\left\{ [\mathbf{S}_h^{(r)}]_{\tau', t'} \right\}}{\sum_{\nu=1}^{T'} \exp\!\left\{ [\mathbf{S}_h^{(r)}]_{\nu, t'} \right\}}, \quad \forall \tau', t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}.
  \label{eq:bidirectional_attention_weights}
\end{align}
Multiplying the value matrix $\mathbf{V}_h^{(r)}$ with $\mathbf{A}_h^{(r)}$ produces the head output $\mathbf{O}_h^{(r)} = \mathbf{V}_h^{(r)} \mathbf{A}_h^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{head}} \times T'}$. Concatenating the outputs of all $N_{\mathrm{head}}$ heads along the feature dimension and applying an output projection with a residual connection yields the intermediate representation $\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{(r)} = \begin{bmatrix} \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_1^{(r)} & \cdots & \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{T'}^{(r)} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$:
\begin{align}
  \widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{(r)} = \mathbf{H}^{(r-1)} + \mathbf{W}_O^{(r)}
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{O}_1^{(r)} \\
    \vdots \\
    \mathbf{O}_{N_{\mathrm{head}}}^{(r)}
  \end{bmatrix}
  + \mathbf{b}_O^{(r)} \mathbf{1}_{T'}^{\mathrm{T}},
  \label{eq:mha_residual}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_O^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times D_{\mathrm{emb}}}$ and $\mathbf{b}_O^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$.

\paragraph{Position-Wise Feed-Forward Sub-Block}
In the second residual sub-block, each column $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{t'}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$ of $\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{(r)}$ is normalized via \gls{LN} and passed through a two-layer position-wise \gls{FFN} consisting of two point-wise ($1 \times 1$) convolutions separated by a \gls{ReLU} activation function, yielding $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r)} = \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{t'}^{(r)} + \mathbf{W}_2^{(r)} \mathrm{ReLU}\!\left\{ \mathbf{W}_1^{(r)} \mathrm{LN}\!\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{t'}^{(r)}\right\} + \mathbf{b}_1^{(r)} \right\} + \mathbf{b}_2^{(r)},
  \label{eq:ffn_residual}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_1^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{FFN}} \times D_{\mathrm{emb}}}$ and $\mathbf{b}_1^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{FFN}} \times 1}$ expand the representation to an inner hidden dimension $D_{\mathrm{FFN}}$, $\mathrm{ReLU}\{x\} = \max(0, x)$ is applied element-wise, and $\mathbf{W}_2^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times D_{\mathrm{FFN}}}$ and $\mathbf{b}_2^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$ project the representation back to dimension $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$. During training, dropout regularization with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}} \in [0, 1)$ is applied after the sinusoidal \gls{PE}~\cite{vaswani2017attention}, to the attention weights $\mathbf{A}_h^{(r)}$ and after the \gls{MHA} output projection~\cite{cornell2022overlapped,vaswani2017attention}, and after the \gls{ReLU} activation in the \gls{FFN}~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}.

\subsubsection{Output Projection, Temporal Upsampling, and Sliding-Window Inference}
\label{sec:output_stage}
After the $R$-th \gls{TE} block, a final point-wise ($1 \times 1$) convolution maps each $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$-dimensional vector $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(R)}$ at subsampled frame $t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}$ to a vector of $(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1)$ unnormalized class logits:
\begin{align}
  \bm{\ell}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{sub})} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}} \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(R)} + \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times 1},
  \label{eq:output_conv}
\end{align}
with $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times D_{\mathrm{emb}}}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times 1}$, forming $\mathbf{L}^{(\mathrm{sub})} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\ell}_1^{(\mathrm{sub})} & \cdots & \bm{\ell}_{T'}^{(\mathrm{sub})} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times T'}$.

When temporal subsampling is active ($T_{\mathrm{sub}} > 1$), $\mathbf{L}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ is stretched (upsampled) by a factor of $T_{\mathrm{sub}}$ to restore the native \gls{STFT} frame resolution $T$~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}. Recall from~\Cref{eq:causal_pool} that each subsampled frame index $t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}$ is anchored at native \gls{STFT} frame $(t'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1$. For any native frame $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$, let
\begin{align}
  t'_t = \left\lfloor \frac{t - 1}{T_{\mathrm{sub}}} \right\rfloor + 1 \in \{1, \dots, T'\}, \quad \eta_t = \frac{(t - 1) \bmod T_{\mathrm{sub}}}{T_{\mathrm{sub}}}
  \label{eq:upsample_index_offset}
\end{align}
denote the preceding subsampled anchor index and the normalized fractional offset $\eta_t \in [0, (T_{\mathrm{sub}}-1)/T_{\mathrm{sub}}]$ toward the subsequent anchor frame, respectively. In the non-causal baseline, because future frames within the segment are accessible, each full-resolution logit vector $\bm{\ell}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times 1}$ is obtained via piecewise linear interpolation between the bracketing subsampled logit vectors $\bm{\ell}_{t'_t}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ and $\bm{\ell}_{\min(t'_t + 1, T')}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$:
\begin{align}
  \bm{\ell}_t = (1 - \eta_t)\,\bm{\ell}_{t'_t}^{(\mathrm{sub})} + \eta_t\,\bm{\ell}_{\min(t'_t + 1, T')}^{(\mathrm{sub})}, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, T\},
  \label{eq:linear_upsampling}
\end{align}
which passes through $\bm{\ell}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ at every anchor frame $t = (t'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1$ ($\eta_t = 0$) and clamps to $\bm{\ell}_{T'}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ for any trailing boundary frames beyond the last anchor $(T'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1$, yielding the full-resolution logit matrix $\mathbf{L} = \begin{bmatrix} \bm{\ell}_1 & \cdots & \bm{\ell}_T \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times T}$ (which reduces to $\mathbf{L} = \mathbf{L}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ for $T_{\mathrm{sub}} = 1$).

During inference on long continuous recordings, Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} process the audio stream using an overlapping sliding window of length $T_{\mathrm{win}}$ frames (corresponding to \SI{3.0}{\second}) advanced by a window hop of $T_{\mathrm{hop}}$ frames (\SI{50}{\percent} overlap, i.e., \SI{1.5}{\second} shift and $T_{\mathrm{ov}} = T_{\mathrm{win}} - T_{\mathrm{hop}}$ overlapping frames). For any global time frame $t$ falling within the overlap region of two consecutive windows $w$ and $w+1$ (with local window frame indices $t_w \in \{T_{\mathrm{hop}}+1, \dots, T_{\mathrm{win}}\}$ and $t_{w+1} = t_w - T_{\mathrm{hop}} \in \{1, \dots, T_{\mathrm{ov}}\}$, respectively), the unnormalized logits of the overlapping blocks are averaged prior to softmax normalization:
\begin{align}
  \bar{\bm{\ell}}_t = \frac{1}{2}\left( \bm{\ell}_{t_w}^{(w)} + \bm{\ell}_{t_{w+1}}^{(w+1)} \right) \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times 1},
  \label{eq:sliding_window_logit_avg}
\end{align}
whereas non-overlapping boundary frames retain their single-window logit vector $\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t = \bm{\ell}_t$. Applying the softmax operator to $\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t$ yields the estimated frame-wise posterior probability vector $\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_t \in [0, 1]^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times 1}$:
\begin{align}
  \widehat{\mathbf{p}}_t = \mathrm{softmax}\{\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t\} = \begin{bmatrix} \widehat{p}_{0,t} & \widehat{p}_{1,t} & \cdots & \widehat{p}_{K_{\mathrm{max}},t} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}},
  \label{eq:softmax_posteriors}
\end{align}
stacked as $\widehat{\mathbf{P}} = \begin{bmatrix} \widehat{\mathbf{p}}_1 & \cdots & \widehat{\mathbf{p}}_T \end{bmatrix} \in [0, 1]^{(K_{\mathrm{max}} + 1) \times T}$, where the estimated posterior probability of $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K_{\mathrm{max}}\}$ active sound sources at time frame $t$ is
\begin{align}
  \widehat{p}_{k,t} = \frac{\exp\{[\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t]_{k+1}\}}{\sum_{\kappa=0}^{K_{\mathrm{max}}} \exp\{[\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t]_{\kappa+1}\}}.
  \label{eq:class_posterior_k}
\end{align}
Finally, the frame-wise source count estimate $\widehat{K}_t$ is obtained as the class index maximizing the posterior probability:
\begin{align}
  \widehat{K}_t = \argmax_{k \in \{0, 1, \dots, K_{\mathrm{max}}\}} \widehat{p}_{k,t}.
  \label{eq:argmax_source_count}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Strictly Causal Adaptation for Online Processing}
\label{sec:strictly_causal_adaptation}
While the baseline system of Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} achieves strong performance by exploiting future information at four distinct points in the pipeline (the $+T_{\mathrm{c}}$ look-ahead frames in the cat-pool front-end~\Cref{eq:causal_cat}, the unmasked bidirectional self-attention across the entire window~\Cref{eq:attention_scores,eq:bidirectional_attention_weights}, the piecewise linear interpolation with the future subsampled anchor frame $\bm{\ell}_{t'_t+1}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ during temporal upsampling~\Cref{eq:linear_upsampling}, and the overlapping sliding-window logit averaging with the subsequent window~\Cref{eq:sliding_window_logit_avg}), real-time streaming applications require strict compliance with the frame-level causality constraint of~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition} ($\tau \le t$). To support both Cornell's non-causal baseline and strictly causal online operation within a single unified architecture, we introduce a boolean causality configuration flag. When strict causality is enabled, the four non-causal operations are modified as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Causal Past-Only Context Concatenation:} Future look-ahead frames in~\Cref{eq:causal_cat} are omitted so that each frame $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ is stacked exclusively with its $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ preceding frames:
  \begin{align}
    \tilde{\mathbf{z}}_t^{(\mathrm{causal})} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_{t - T_{\mathrm{c}}}^{\mathrm{T}} & \cdots & \mathbf{z}_{t-1}^{\mathrm{T}} & \mathbf{z}_t^{\mathrm{T}} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat}} \times 1},
    \label{eq:strict_causal_cat}
  \end{align}
  where $D_{\mathrm{cat}} = N_{\mathrm{ctx}} D$ with $N_{\mathrm{ctx}} = T_{\mathrm{c}} + 1$, and $\mathbf{z}_{\tau} = \mathbf{0}_{D \times 1}$ for $\tau \le 0$. Subsampling via~\Cref{eq:causal_pool} then evaluates $\tilde{\mathbf{z}}_t^{(\mathrm{causal})}$ at anchor frames $(t'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1$, each depending exclusively on past frames $\tau \le (t'-1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1$.

  \item \textbf{Causal Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention:} To ensure that the query at subsampled frame $t'$ attends exclusively to key and value vectors from current and past frames $\tau' \le t'$, an upper-triangular causal mask matrix $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{causal}} \in \{0, -\infty\}^{T' \times T'}$ with entries
  \begin{align}
    [\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{causal}}]_{\tau', t'} =
    \begin{cases}
      0, & \text{if } \tau' \le t', \\
      -\infty, & \text{if } \tau' > t',
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:causal_mask}
  \end{align}
  is added to the scaled dot-product score matrix in~\Cref{eq:attention_scores}:
  \begin{align}
    \mathbf{S}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})} = \frac{\left(\mathbf{K}_h^{(r)}\right)^{\mathrm{T}} \mathbf{Q}_h^{(r)}}{\sqrt{D_{\mathrm{head}}}} + \mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{causal}} \in \mathbb{R}^{T' \times T'}.
    \label{eq:causal_attention_scores}
  \end{align}
  Applying the column-wise softmax yields the strictly causal attention weight matrix $\mathbf{A}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})} \in [0, 1]^{T' \times T'}$, with $[\mathbf{A}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})}]_{\tau', t'} = 0$ for $\tau' > t'$ and
  \begin{align}
    [\mathbf{A}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})}]_{\tau', t'} = \frac{\exp\!\left\{ [\mathbf{S}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})}]_{\tau', t'} \right\}}{\sum_{\nu=1}^{t'} \exp\!\left\{ [\mathbf{S}_h^{(r,\mathrm{c})}]_{\nu, t'} \right\}}, \quad \forall \tau' \le t',
    \label{eq:causal_attention_weights}
  \end{align}
  guaranteeing zero information leakage from future frames $\tau' > t'$.

  \item \textbf{Causal Zero-Order-Hold Temporal Upsampling:} When temporal subsampling is active ($T_{\mathrm{sub}} > 1$), linear interpolation via~\Cref{eq:linear_upsampling} requires the subsequent subsampled logit vector $\bm{\ell}_{t'_t + 1}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$, which is anchored at future \gls{STFT} frame $t'_t T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1 > t$ whenever $\eta_t > 0$ (introducing up to $T_{\mathrm{sub}} - 1$ frames of future look-ahead). To strictly preserve causality without accessing future subsampled frames, we replace linear interpolation with a causal zero-order hold ($\eta_t = 0$), repeating the most recent causal subsampled logit vector $\bm{\ell}_{t'_t}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ over the current and subsequent $T_{\mathrm{sub}} - 1$ frames:
  \begin{align}
    \bm{\ell}_t^{(\mathrm{causal})} = \bm{\ell}_{t'_t}^{(\mathrm{sub})} = \bm{\ell}_{\lfloor (t-1)/T_{\mathrm{sub}} \rfloor + 1}^{(\mathrm{sub})}, \quad \forall t \in \{1, \dots, T\}.
    \label{eq:causal_upsampling}
  \end{align}
  Because $(t'_t - 1)T_{\mathrm{sub}} + 1 \le t$ holds for all $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$,~\Cref{eq:causal_upsampling} guarantees zero future look-ahead in compliance with~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition} (updating the output once every $T_{\mathrm{sub}}$ frames, or at every frame when $T_{\mathrm{sub}} = 1$).

  \item \textbf{Causal Burn-In Sliding-Window Evaluation:} During continuous evaluation on sequential overlapping windows of length $T_{\mathrm{win}}$ frames advanced by $T_{\mathrm{hop}}$ frames (with overlap $T_{\mathrm{ov}} = T_{\mathrm{win}} - T_{\mathrm{hop}}$), averaging logits with future windows via~\Cref{eq:sliding_window_logit_avg} would violate causality. Instead, in causal mode, the first window ($w = 1$) outputs predictions over its entire span $t_1 \in \{1, \dots, T_{\mathrm{win}}\}$, while every subsequent window ($w \ge 2$) uses its initial $T_{\mathrm{ov}}$ overlapping frames exclusively as a causal past-context warmup (burn-in) buffer for self-attention and discards their logits, emitting predictions strictly over the newly advancing non-overlapping tail $t_w \in \{T_{\mathrm{ov}}+1, \dots, T_{\mathrm{win}}\}$ with $\bar{\bm{\ell}}_t = \bm{\ell}_{t_w}^{(w)}$.
\end{enumerate}

The unified forward pass of the single-stream Transformer covering both the non-causal baseline and the strictly causal configuration is summarized in~\Cref{alg:causal_transformer}. All configurable architectural hyperparameters, together with the search space and selected default values reported by Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}, are compiled in~\Cref{tab:transformer_hyperparams}.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{Unified Single-Stream Transformer Forward Pass (Baseline and Strictly Causal Modes)}
  \label{alg:causal_transformer}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \REQUIRE Input feature matrix $\mathbf{Z} = [\mathbf{z}_1, \dots, \mathbf{z}_T] \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times T}$; causality flag $\texttt{causal} \in \{\texttt{False}, \texttt{True}\}$; hyperparameters $T_{\mathrm{c}}, T_{\mathrm{sub}}, D_{\mathrm{emb}}, R, N_{\mathrm{head}}, D_{\mathrm{FFN}}, p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ (\Cref{tab:transformer_hyperparams})
    \ENSURE Frame-level logit matrix $\mathbf{L} \in \mathbb{R}^{(K_{\mathrm{max}}+1) \times T}$, posterior probability matrix $\widehat{\mathbf{P}} \in [0, 1]^{(K_{\mathrm{max}}+1) \times T}$, and source count estimates $\{\widehat{K}_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$
    \STATE \textbf{Cat-pool front-end:} If $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{False}$, zero-pad boundary frames outside $\{1, \dots, T\}$ and stack $2T_{\mathrm{c}}+1$ bidirectional frames via~\Cref{eq:causal_cat} ($D_{\mathrm{cat}} = (2T_{\mathrm{c}}+1)D$); else zero-pad $\tau \le 0$ and stack $T_{\mathrm{c}}+1$ past-only frames via~\Cref{eq:strict_causal_cat} ($D_{\mathrm{cat}} = (T_{\mathrm{c}}+1)D$). Subsample by $T_{\mathrm{sub}}$ via~\Cref{eq:causal_pool} to form $\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat}} \times T'}$ with $T' = \lfloor (T - 1) / T_{\mathrm{sub}} \rfloor + 1$.
    \STATE \textbf{Input normalization \& bottleneck:} For $t' = 1, \dots, T'$, apply feature-wise \acrshort{LN}~\Cref{eq:layernorm_def} to $\mathbf{u}_{t'}$ and point-wise projection~\Cref{eq:bottleneck_conv} to obtain $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0,\mathrm{in})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$.
    \STATE \textbf{Positional encoding:} Compute $\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{PE}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$ via~\Cref{eq:pe_sin,eq:pe_cos} and set $\mathbf{H}^{(0)} = \mathbf{H}^{(0,\mathrm{in})} + \mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{PE}}$ via~\Cref{eq:pos_enc_add} (apply dropout with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ during training).
    \FOR{$r = 1$ \TO $R$}
      \STATE Normalize columns of $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1)}$ via~\Cref{eq:layernorm_def} to obtain $\bar{\mathbf{H}}^{(r-1)}$.
      \FOR{$h = 1$ \TO $N_{\mathrm{head}}$}
        \STATE Project $\mathbf{Q}_h^{(r)}, \mathbf{K}_h^{(r)}, \mathbf{V}_h^{(r)}$ via~\Cref{eq:mha_q,eq:mha_k,eq:mha_v}.
        \STATE If $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{False}$, compute unmasked scores $\mathbf{S}_h^{(r)}$ and bidirectional weights $\mathbf{A}_h^{(r)}$ via~\Cref{eq:attention_scores,eq:bidirectional_attention_weights}; else apply $\mathbf{M}_{\mathrm{causal}}$ via~\Cref{eq:causal_mask,eq:causal_attention_scores,eq:causal_attention_weights} (apply attention dropout with probability $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ during training).
        \STATE Compute head output $\mathbf{O}_h^{(r)} = \mathbf{V}_h^{(r)} \mathbf{A}_h^{(r)}$.
      \ENDFOR
      \STATE Concatenate $\{\mathbf{O}_h^{(r)}\}_{h=1}^{N_{\mathrm{head}}}$, apply $\mathbf{W}_O^{(r)}, \mathbf{b}_O^{(r)}$ (with training dropout $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$), and add residual $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1)}$ via~\Cref{eq:mha_residual} to obtain $\widetilde{\mathbf{H}}^{(r)}$.
      \STATE For $t' = 1, \dots, T'$, apply \acrshort{LN}~\Cref{eq:layernorm_def} and the two-layer \acrshort{FFN} (with training dropout $p_{\mathrm{drop}}$ after \acrshort{ReLU}) plus residual addition via~\Cref{eq:ffn_residual} to obtain $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r)}$.
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE \textbf{Output projection \& upsampling:} Compute subsampled logits $\bm{\ell}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{sub})}$ for $t' = 1, \dots, T'$ via~\Cref{eq:output_conv}, restore native resolution $T$ via piecewise linear interpolation~\Cref{eq:linear_upsampling} if $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{False}$ or causal zero-order hold~\Cref{eq:causal_upsampling} if $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{True}$, apply sliding-window stitching (\Cref{eq:sliding_window_logit_avg} if $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{False}$, or causal burn-in trimming if $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{True}$), and compute $\widehat{\mathbf{p}}_t$ and $\widehat{K}_t$ via~\Cref{eq:softmax_posteriors,eq:class_posterior_k,eq:argmax_source_count}.
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When combining single-channel spectral cues with multi-microphone spatial cues in the baseline Transformer architecture, Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} investigated two distinct fusion strategies: early fusion via input concatenation and late fusion via \gls{FiLM}~\cite{perez2018film}, both depicted in~\Cref{fig:feature_fusion}.

Let $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{spec}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{z}_1^{(\mathrm{spec})} & \cdots & \mathbf{z}_T^{(\mathrm{spec})} \end{bmatrix} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{spec}} \times T}$ denote the single-channel spectral feature sequence extracted from a reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (e.g., $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$ with $D_{\mathrm{spec}} = 3F$, or $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$ with $D_{\mathrm{spec}} = D_{\mathrm{mel}}$). In the baseline multi-channel framework of~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}, pairwise spatial features extracted across $P$ selected microphone pairs (such as $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{CSIPD}} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times 2F \times T}$ or $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{PSC}} \in \mathbb{R}^{P \times 3F \times T}$) are flattened across the pair dimension into a single spatial feature vector per time frame $t$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{z}_t^{(\mathrm{spat})} = \begin{bmatrix} \left(\mathbf{z}_{1,t}^{(\mathrm{spat})}\right)^{\mathrm{T}} & \cdots & \left(\mathbf{z}_{P,t}^{(\mathrm{spat})}\right)^{\mathrm{T}} \end{bmatrix}^{\mathrm{T}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{spat}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:flattened_spatial_vec}
\end{align}
with $D_{\mathrm{spat}} = P \cdot D_{\mathrm{pair}}$ (whereas array-level coherence features such as $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{GMSC}}$ and $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{wGMSC}}$ directly satisfy $C=1$ with $D_{\mathrm{spat}} \in \{F, 2F\}$). Stacking $\mathbf{z}_t^{(\mathrm{spat})}$ across all $T$ frames yields $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{spat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{spat}} \times T}$.

To align the temporal resolution of both streams under arbitrary subsampling factor $T_{\mathrm{sub}}$, $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{spec}}$ and $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{spat}}$ independently undergo the cat-pool operation in~\Cref{eq:causal_cat,eq:causal_pool} (or its causal counterpart~\Cref{eq:strict_causal_cat,eq:causal_pool} when $\texttt{causal} = \texttt{True}$) using identical context parameter $T_{\mathrm{c}}$ and subsampling factor $T_{\mathrm{sub}}$, yielding $\mathbf{u}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spec})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} \times 1}$ and $\mathbf{u}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}} \times 1}$, followed by separate feature-wise \gls{LN} as in~\Cref{eq:layernorm_def}:
\begin{align}
  \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spec})} &= \mathrm{LN}\!\left\{\mathbf{u}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spec})}\right\} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} \times 1}, \label{eq:ln_spec_stream} \\
  \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})} &= \mathrm{LN}\!\left\{\mathbf{u}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})}\right\} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}} \times 1}, \label{eq:ln_spat_stream}
\end{align}
where $D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} = N_{\mathrm{ctx}} D_{\mathrm{spec}}$ and $D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}} = N_{\mathrm{ctx}} D_{\mathrm{spat}}$ (with $N_{\mathrm{ctx}} = 2T_{\mathrm{c}} + 1$ in the non-causal baseline and $N_{\mathrm{ctx}} = T_{\mathrm{c}} + 1$ in causal mode), forming the matrices $\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathrm{spec}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} \times T'}$ and $\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathrm{spat}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}} \times T'}$.

\subsubsection{Early Fusion via Input Concatenation}
\label{sec:early_fusion}
As shown in~\Cref{fig:feature_fusion}(a), early fusion concatenates the normalized spectral and spatial context vectors along the feature axis at each subsampled time frame $t' \in \{1, \dots, T'\}$:
\begin{align}
  \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{early})} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spec})} \\
    \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{(D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} + D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}}) \times 1}.
  \label{eq:early_fusion_concat}
\end{align}
The bottleneck point-wise ($1 \times 1$) convolution in~\Cref{eq:bottleneck_conv} is then applied directly to the joint vector $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{early})}$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(0,\mathrm{in})} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}^{(\mathrm{early})} \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{early})} + \mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}}^{(\mathrm{early})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1},
  \label{eq:early_fusion_bottleneck}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{in}}^{(\mathrm{early})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times (D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}} + D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}})}$ and $\mathbf{b}_{\mathrm{in}}^{(\mathrm{early})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$. All subsequent stages (sinusoidal \gls{PE}, the $R$ \gls{TE} blocks, and the output classification head with causal upsampling) remain identical to~\Cref{sec:causal_transformer_base}. While computationally simple, early fusion forces both feature modalities through a single $D_{\mathrm{emb}}$-dimensional bottleneck in the first layer, which can compress high-dimensional pairwise spatial representations when $D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}} \gg D_{\mathrm{cat,spec}}$~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}.

\subsubsection{Late Fusion via Feature-Wise Linear Modulation (FiLM)}
\label{sec:late_fusion_film}
To avoid compressing spatial information in the initial bottleneck and to provide spatial cues directly to deeper layers, late fusion injects $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})}$ prior to each \gls{TE} block $r \in \{1, \dots, R\}$ via \gls{FiLM}~\cite{perez2018film,cornell2022overlapped}, as depicted in~\Cref{fig:feature_fusion}(b). In this configuration, the initial embedding sequence $\mathbf{H}^{(0)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$ is computed exclusively from the normalized spectral stream $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spec})}$ via~\Cref{eq:bottleneck_conv,eq:pos_enc_add}.

At the entrance of each \gls{TE} block $r \in \{1, \dots, R\}$, a layer-specific \gls{FiLM} generator projects the normalized spatial conditioning vector $\bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})}$ into a feature-wise scaling vector $\bm{\gamma}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$ and shifting vector $\bm{\beta}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$:
\begin{align}
  \bm{\gamma}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} &= \mathbf{W}_{\gamma}^{(r)} \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})} + \mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}, \label{eq:film_gamma} \\
  \bm{\beta}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} &= \mathbf{W}_{\beta}^{(r)} \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})} + \mathbf{b}_{\beta}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}, \label{eq:film_beta}
\end{align}
with learnable projection matrices $\mathbf{W}_{\gamma}^{(r)}, \mathbf{W}_{\beta}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times D_{\mathrm{cat,spat}}}$ and bias vectors $\mathbf{b}_{\gamma}^{(r)}, \mathbf{b}_{\beta}^{(r)} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}$. The hidden representation $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r-1)}$ entering the $r$-th \gls{TE} block is then modulated via an element-wise affine transformation~\cite{perez2018film}:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r-1,\mathrm{FiLM})} &= \mathrm{FiLM}\!\left\{\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r-1)}, \bar{\mathbf{u}}_{t'}^{(\mathrm{spat})}\right\} \nonumber \\
  &= \bm{\gamma}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} \odot \mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r-1)} + \bm{\beta}_{t'}^{(r,\mathrm{FiLM})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times 1}.
  \label{eq:film_modulation}
\end{align}
Stacking $\mathbf{h}_{t'}^{(r-1,\mathrm{FiLM})}$ across all $T'$ subsampled frames yields the modulated matrix $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1,\mathrm{FiLM})} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_{\mathrm{emb}} \times T'}$, which replaces $\mathbf{H}^{(r-1)}$ as the input to the $r$-th \gls{TE} block in~\Cref{sec:transformer_encoder_blocks}.

\section{Datasets}
\label{sec:datasets}

Table~\ref{tab:datasets_overview} provides an overview of the datasets utilized for training and evaluation.
The datasets fall into three distinct categories, which differ in their generation methodologies and array geometries.
First, the synthetic datasets are entirely simulated using the image source method and generated diffuse noise,
utilizing both uniform circular and randomized planar arrays.
Second, the \gls{BRUDEX} evaluation dataset is constructed using measured \glspl{RIR} and recorded ambient noise,
captured by a dummy head equipped with behind-the-ear and in-ear microphones.

\subsection{Synthetic Dataset}

To provide sufficient training data and to analyze the impact of array geometry and channel count on the performance of the models,
randomized synthetic datasets are constructed.
The acoustic environments are simulated using the image source method
provided by the Pyroomacoustics library~\cite{scheibler2018pyroomacoustics} to generate \glspl{RIR}.
Shoebox rooms are generated with lengths and widths ranging from \SI{4.0}{\meter} to \SI{8.0}{\meter}
and heights between \SI{2.5}{\meter} and \SI{4.0}{\meter}.
The reverberation time ($RT_{60}$) is uniformly sampled between \SI{0.2}{\second} and \SI{0.8}{\second}.
To simulate realistic ambient background noise, diffuse noise is generated using the
ANF-Generator~\cite{habets_ANFgenerator_2021}\footnote{Code: \url{https://github.com/audiolabs/anf-generator}}
by spatializing single-channel noise recordings from the DEMAND database~\cite{thiemann2013demand}.
The DEMAND noise environments are partitioned by their acoustic categories:
Domestic, Nature, Office, and Public environments for training,
Transportation environments for validation,
and Street environments for testing.

The acoustic scenarios are constructed using clean speech utterances
from the LibriSpeech corpus~\cite{panayotov2015librispeech}.
Specifically, the \texttt{train-clean-360} split is used for training,
the \texttt{dev-clean} split for validation, and the \texttt{test-clean} split for testing.
Each simulated acoustic scenario has a duration of \SI{120.0}{\second}
and features up to $4$ conversational partners.
The speakers randomly start and stop talking, creating highly overlapping speech.
A scenario typically begins with a short period containing only background noise,
lasting between \SI{1.0}{\second} and \SI{5.0}{\second}, before the speakers begin their conversation.
The onset and offset of speech for each conversational partner occur at random intervals,
with the duration between any two consecutive conversational events uniformly sampled
between \SI{1.0}{\second} and \SI{10.0}{\second}. This dynamic
continuously alters the number of concurrently active speakers throughout the scenario.
For these synthetic datasets, the speakers maintain a minimum distance of \SI{0.5}{\meter}
from each other and the microphone array, and are separated by a minimum direction
of arrival of \SI{30}{\degree} relative to the center of the array.

To investigate generalization capabilities across different hardware configurations,
the four distinct synthetic datasets detailed in Table~\ref{tab:datasets_overview} are constructed.
These datasets are defined along two dimensions:
the array geometry (uniform circular arrays with a fixed radius of \SI{10}{\centi\meter}
versus randomized planar geometries uniformly distributed within a \SI{20}{\centi\meter} $\times$ \SI{20}{\centi\meter} square area)
and the number of microphone channels
(a fixed count of $8$ microphones versus a variable count uniformly sampled between $3$ and $8$ channels).

\subsection{BRUDEX}

The \gls{BRUDEX} database~\cite{fejgin2023brudex} contains \glspl{RIR} and multi-channel noise recordings
captured in various reverberant environments using a dummy head (and external microphones, which are not used in this study).
The dummy head is equipped with both \gls{BTE} hearing aids,
featuring a front and rear microphone on each ear,
as well as in-ear microphones positioned within the ear canals.

Similar to the synthetic dataset, the dynamic acoustic scenarios are constructed
using clean speech utterances from the LibriSpeech corpus~\cite{panayotov2015librispeech},
utilizing the exact same stochastic conversational dynamic
to simulate up to $4$ concurrently active speakers over a duration of \SI{120.0}{\second}.
The physical microphone array configuration is randomly selected for each scenario
from a subset of available array geometries:
all \gls{BTE} and in-ear microphones combined ($6$ channels),
only the \gls{BTE} microphones ($4$ channels),
only the front or only the rear \gls{BTE} microphones ($2$ channels each),
the in-ear microphones combined with either the front or the rear \gls{BTE} microphones ($4$ channels each),
and asymmetric configurations combining the \gls{BTE} microphones of one ear
with the in-ear microphone of the opposite ear ($3$ channels).
This geometric diversity results in varying numbers of input channels
and distinct spatial coordinates.

The data is partitioned into distinct training, validation, and testing sets with non-overlapping spatial and acoustic parameters.
For the spatial distribution of the active speakers, the directions of arrival are strictly partitioned.
The training set utilizes directions of arrival of
\SI{-30}{\degree}, \SI{60}{\degree}, \SI{-90}{\degree}, \SI{120}{\degree}, \SI{-150}{\degree}, and \SI{180}{\degree}.
The validation set utilizes \SI{30}{\degree}, \SI{-120}{\degree}, and \SI{150}{\degree}.
Finally, the testing set utilizes \SI{0}{\degree}, \SI{-60}{\degree}, and \SI{90}{\degree}.
Furthermore, the environmental noise conditions are strictly separated:
The training set is corrupted by babble and white noise,
the validation set by babble noise,
and the testing set by cafeteria noise.
Across all scenarios, the \gls{SNR} ranges continuously between \SI{5.0}{\decibel} and \SI{20.0}{\decibel},
and the relative speaker powers are distributed within an interval of \SI{-5.0}{\decibel} to \SI{5.0}{\decibel}.
The acoustic environments are rendered using the \textit{low} reverberation condition
(median reverberation time $RT_{60}\approx \SI{310}{\milli\s}$) provided by the \gls{BRUDEX} database.

The ground truth speech activity in this simulated dataset is defined purely based on acoustic power, derived by applying
an energy threshold of \SI{-30}{\decibel} to the clean speech signal components.
Silences shorter than \SI{1.0}{\second} are neglected, ensuring continuous speech activity segments during fluent speech.

\subsection{AMI meeting corpus}

The \gls{AMI} meeting corpus~\cite{mccowan2005ami} contains approximately \SI{100}{\hour} of recorded meetings,
featuring sessions with $3$ to $5$ participants, though at most $4$ speakers are ever simultaneously active.
The recordings were conducted in three distinct instrumented meeting rooms (Edinburgh, Idiap, and TNO),
resulting in heterogeneous distant microphone array configurations across the corpus.
The Edinburgh room features two $8$-channel uniform circular arrays with a radius of \SI{10}{\centi\meter} (Array $1$ and Array $2$).
The Idiap room contains one $8$-channel uniform circular array on the table (Array $1$)
and one $4$-channel uniform circular array mounted on the ceiling (Array $2$).
Finally, the TNO room is equipped with an $8$-channel uniform circular array on the table (Array $1$)
and a $10$-channel linear array (Array $2$).

The data preparation follows the methodology proposed by Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped}.
The ground truth \gls{SAD} labels are derived directly
from the official manual word-level annotations (\texttt{ami\_public\_manual\_1.6.2}),
which were reliably obtained by human annotators utilizing the individual close-talk headset microphones.
The division of the recorded meetings into training, validation, and testing sets strictly adheres
to the official \gls{AMI} Full-corpus-ASR partition, which includes meetings from all three rooms.

During training, a dynamic data augmentation strategy is employed
to expose the model to a well-distributed variety of overlap conditions.
For \SI{50}{\percent} of the training data, unaltered contiguous chunks of \SI{7.0}{\second}
are sampled from random temporal positions within the training meetings.
For the remaining \SI{50}{\percent} of the training data,
dynamically augmented chunks of \SI{7.0}{\second} are generated.
These augmented chunks are constructed by sampling isolated single-speaker chunks
from different meetings and mixing the isolated single-speaker chunks together.
The augmented chunks contain $2$, $3$, or $4$ concurrent speakers,
each with a probability of approximately \SI{33}{\percent}.
To simulate acoustic variations, each additional single-speaker chunk
is mixed with a relative \gls{SIR} shift.
The relative \gls{SIR} shift is drawn from a Gaussian distribution
($\mu = \SI{0}{\decibel}$, $\sigma = \SI{4.0}{\decibel}$) bounded between \SI{-10}{\decibel} and \SI{10}{\decibel}.
To prevent numerical overflow, if the peak amplitude of the augmented chunk exceeds $0.99$,
the augmented chunk is uniformly scaled to a target peak amplitude uniformly drawn
from the interval $[0.5, 0.99]$. 

For validation and testing, contiguous chunks are extracted sequentially across each recording using two supported sliding-window protocols:
(i) in the non-causal baseline configuration of Cornell et al.~\cite{cornell2022overlapped} ($\texttt{causal} = \texttt{False}$, trained on \SI{5.0}{\second} chunks), evaluation windows of \SI{3.0}{\second} are advanced with a hop length of \SI{1.5}{\second} (\SI{50}{\percent} overlap) and overlapping frame logits are averaged via~\Cref{eq:sliding_window_logit_avg};
(ii) in the strictly causal configuration ($\texttt{causal} = \texttt{True}$, trained on \SI{7.0}{\second} chunks), evaluation windows of \SI{7.0}{\second} are advanced with a hop length of \SI{5.0}{\second} (\SI{2.0}{\second} overlap), where the initial \SI{2.0}{\second} of each subsequent chunk serve as a causal past-context burn-in buffer and are discarded prior to scoring (\Cref{sec:strictly_causal_adaptation}).


\section{System Design Space and Configuration Dimensions}
\label{sec:design_space}

Having introduced the extracted acoustic representations (\Cref{sec:feature_extraction}), the Transformer backbone in both its non-causal baseline and strictly causal forms (\Cref{sec:proposed_architecture}), and the evaluation corpora (\Cref{sec:datasets}), we structure the family of investigated sound source counting systems along four primary system-characterizing dimensions. Crucially, these internal system dimensions are distinguished from the evaluation datasets used at test time, which probe generalization regimes rather than defining the model itself. An overview of all dimensions, available options, and standardized constants is provided in~\Cref{tab:design_dimensions}.

\subsection{Dim.~0: Channel Scope and Operational Regime}
\label{sec:dim_scope}
The fundamental bifurcation in the design space separates \emph{single-channel} systems ($M = 1$) from \emph{multi-channel} systems ($M \ge 2$). Single-channel estimators operate solely on spectral cues ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$ or $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}}^{(\mathrm{ref})}$) extracted from a randomly sampled reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$, meaning variable microphone counts and spatial array geometry do not arise. Multi-channel estimators instead exploit inter-microphone spatial information across $M \ge 2$ channels and operate under one of two regimes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fixed-array regime:} Both the microphone count $M$ and the microphone indexing order (including a fixed reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}} = 1$) remain constant and matched between training and testing, permitting architectures with fixed input dimensions tied to a specific array layout.
  \item \textbf{Variable-$M$ and permutation-invariant regime:} The architecture supports arbitrary microphone counts $M$, microphone channels are randomly permuted during both training and testing, and whenever a single-channel spectral feature is paired with spatial features, its reference microphone $m_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is selected randomly per example.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dim.~1: Input Feature Modalities and Array Geometry}
\label{sec:dim_features}
Multi-channel systems may ingest purely spectral features ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{STFT}}$, $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{mel}}$, or their single-channel counterparts), purely spatial features, or a combination of both. Among spatial representations, pairwise features ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{CSIPD}}$, $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{PSC}}$) are always computed over the complete set of $P = M(M-1)/2$ microphone pairs without geometry-specific pair sub-selection, whereas array-condensed coherence features ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{GMSC}}$, $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{wGMSC}}$) natively collapse the $M$-channel covariance structure into $C = 1$ stream.

Optionally, planar 2D array geometry may be supplied as a third input modality. To ensure translation invariance across arbitrary room placements, coordinates are represented in relative form, either as centroid-centered 2D microphone positions $\tilde{\mathbf{p}}_m = \mathbf{p}_m - \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\mathbf{p}_j \in \mathbb{R}^2$ (with arbitrary coordinate-axis orientation), pairwise baseline difference vectors $\mathbf{v}_{ij} = \mathbf{p}_j - \mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$, rotation-invariant pairwise Euclidean distances $d_{ij} = \|\mathbf{v}_{ij}\|_2$, or a combination thereof, transformed via either a compact learnable projection or sinusoidal Fourier \gls{PE}.

\subsection{Dim.~2: DNN Architecture and Modality Interaction}
\label{sec:dim_architecture}
The architectural dimension comprises three coupled sub-dimensions that exhibit strong interactions with the chosen input features and their channel cardinalities ($C \in \{1, M, P\}$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Channel and stream handling:} In the fixed-array regime, multi-channel or pairwise features may be flattened into a fixed-width vector. In the variable-$M$ regime, architectures must be structurally independent of $M$ and permutation-equivariant across channels: either through a single-stream Transformer ($C = 1$) fed by array-condensed features ($\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{GMSC}}$, $\mathbf{Z}_{\mathrm{wGMSC}}$) or early channel pooling, or through $C \in \{M, P\}$ parallel shared-weight Transformer streams interleaved with permutation-equivariant cross-channel sharing layers (e.g., \gls{TAC} or cross-channel self-attention) and final masked stream pooling.
  \item \textbf{Spectral--spatial combination and cardinality bridging:} When combining spectral and spatial features, modalities may be merged via early input concatenation, unidirectional conditioning (such as layer-wise \gls{FiLM}), or bidirectional cross-conditioning. Whenever pairwise spatial features ($C = P$) are combined with multi-channel spectral streams ($C = M$), paired with a single-channel spectral stream ($C = 1$) in a variable-$M$ model, or reduced to a scalar output in spatial-only systems, the architecture must additionally specify a permutation-equivariant reduction or expansion bridge ($P \to M$, $M \to P$, or $P \to 1$).
  \item \textbf{Geometry injection:} When 2D array geometry is provided, coordinates can be injected via early feature concatenation or via late conditioning (such as \gls{FiLM} modulation of spatial embeddings or conditioning of the cross-channel sharing blocks).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Dim.~3: Training Dataset and Fixed Constants}
\label{sec:dim_training_constants}
The training dataset (Circ8, Rand8, Circ3-8, Rand3-8, \gls{BRUDEX}, or \gls{AMI}) determines the geometric diversity, microphone-count variability, and acoustic domain exposed during optimization, and is intentionally decoupled from the held-out testing corpora. To isolate the impact of the four active dimensions above, the remaining system-level parameters are standardized across the core investigation: the causality regime is explicitly controlled via $\texttt{causal} \in \{\texttt{False}, \texttt{True}\}$ (comparing Cornell's non-causal baseline with $T_{\mathrm{sub}} = 10$, linear upsampling~\Cref{eq:linear_upsampling}, and \SI{50}{\percent}-overlap sliding-window logit averaging against the strictly frame-causal configuration of~\Cref{eq:strict_causality_definition} with causal zero-order hold upsampling~\Cref{eq:causal_upsampling} and causal burn-in chunking), predictions are always restored to the native \gls{STFT} frame resolution $T$, and all models are optimized using standard frame-wise cross-entropy loss (while alternative ordinal or temporally smoothed loss formulations are deferred to a separate study).
